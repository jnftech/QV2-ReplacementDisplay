# Installation instructions

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig0_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="640"></p>

> [!CAUTION]
> **Guide assumes you are reasonably familiar with working inside electronic devices.
> Ensure device is disconnected from mains power (especially in the case of the Q20).
> Author is not responsible for damage to your Quadraverb. Proceed at your own risk!**

> [!TIP]
> This procedure covers both the Quadraverb 2 and the Quadraverb Q20. Differences between the two units will be noted in each step.

### Step 1: Remove Top Cover
There are three screws on the top cover of the unit, as well as three more in the rear.
With these six screws removed, the top cover should slide off towards the rear of the unit. Set the screws and cover aside.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig1-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig1-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig1-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 2: Disconnect all cables from the front bezel assembly
Several cables connect the mainboard to various components of the front panel.
Many of them are keyed PH connectors[^1] which will only seat one way (J14, J15, J16, and J17 - or on the Q20: J15, J16, J17 and J20)
However the two ribbon cables (J5 and J10, or on the Q20: J19 and J18) are unshrouded IDC-style[^2] connectors, <b>which can be connected backwards during reassembly if you are not careful</b>, so take a moment to note the orientation of these two cables before removing them from the mainboard. The first wire of each cable is colored so you can use this as a reference (shown in [Fig 2.2](/_images/fig2-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg)).
Unfortunately these two ribbon cables will typically be hot-glued in place. Carefully use a plastic pry tool or needle-nose pliers to loosen the glue. If you use a metal tool (like I have shown), avoid coming in contact with the mainboard or nearby components, as this could lead to damage.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig2-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig2-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig2-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig2-4_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 3: Remove the front bezel assembly
Carefully flip the unit over, and you will find another three screws on the bottom (similar to the top panel). Remove these three screws and set aside. This will loosen most of what is holding the unit together, so at this point carefully slide away the two mounting brackets and set them aside. The mainboard+bottom/rear panel assembly can be lifted away and set aside, leaving you with full access to the front bezel assembly.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig3-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig3-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig3-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><br/><img src="/_images/fig3-4_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig3-5_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 4: Remove encoder knob/PCB
Remove the plastic knob for the encoder to access the nut that secures the encoder PCB to the front panel. From the rear/inside of the front panel, you will notice a small hole next to the encoder shaft. Using a small long tool (like a jewelers screwdriver) you can use this hole to free the plastic knob by inserting it and pressing forward with a little force. The knob should pop off, and you can now access the the 11mm hex nut and washer securing the encoder in place. Remove the nut and washer. The encoder PCB should now be free to remove, though note the orientation of the PCB for reassembly.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig4-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig4-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig4-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><br/><img src="/_images/fig4-4_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig4-5_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 5: Remove clips/nylon spacers
Several metal clips hold some flat pieces of (I think) nylon plastic spacers. Make a note of where these clips and spacers are set, as you will need to put them back them during reassembly. The clips can be easily removed with needle nose pliers.

***"Why the nylon spacers"*** you ask? Alesis chose to use these spacers to basically "press-fit" these assemblies to the front panel without the use of screws. They are in fact very important, so take care not to lose them. Remember where the spacers go as they have different lengths. For reference, the short spacers sit behind the display PCB closest to the volume/input knobs, the slightly-longer spacers sit behind the encoder, the the long spacers of course sit behind the button PCB assembly - where you want extra support to ensure repeated button presses.

Like some of the connections to the mainboard, hot glue is used in some parts to secure the spacers. Use a pry tool or pliers to remove here as well (same cautions as above). The spacers should come free with similar tools. 

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig5-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig5-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig5-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig5-4_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 6: Slide out the Button PCB assembly
Before proceeding, I must point out the screws you will see in the button and display PCB. For the next few steps, **DO NOT REMOVE THE SCREWS** - it will make getting these items free much easier!
In order to free up the screen assembly, you need to get the button assembly out of the way. With the spacers removed, and the front panel facing downward on your working surface, you should be able to gently lift up on the button PCB by the cable and the outer-facing side, and then slide it out of the front panel.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig6-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig6-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig6-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 7: Remove old display
Now that the buttons are out of the way, you should be able to lift up on the display PCB and gently slide it out of the front panel. At this point you can set aside the rest of the front panel, leaving you with just the old display PCB and its plastic housing.
Before proceeding, it is a good idea to remove the two cables still attached to the display PCB, and (using notes from earlier of where things should go) reattach them to the mainboard for safekeeping. Once again, make note of the orientation of the ribbon cable (the marked line SHOULD be facing the "bottom" of the PCB as shown, but double check just in case any previous service resulted in something different), and carefully remove any hot glue that may be securing the connector in place.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig7-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig7-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig7-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig7-4_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 8: Detach old display PCB from assembly
efore unscrewing, there may be some tape across one side that will interfere with separating the plastic from the PCB. Remove this tape.
With the PCB side facing up, remove the four screws. Before trying to separate the parts, it is a good idea to flip over the assembly and lift from the plastic housing instead of the PCB (shown in [Fig 8.3](/_images/fig8-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg)).

> [!WARNING]
> The backlight assembly is made of several pieces that are now free to move around and may become loose, so it's best to keep it in this face-up orientation until stored.

We will reuse the old display's plastic housing, so set it aside for now, and your work surface should be left with the old acrylic display cover (with the VU dB markings), LCD/backlight, and the old PCB. Take a moment to carefully secure and store these items. Loose-fitting rubber bands (to keep the LCD/Backlight pieces together), resealable plastic bag and a small padded box work well to keep the old LCD assembly together.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig8-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig8-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig8-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig8-4_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 9: Slightly modify plastic housing
**This is unfortunately the ONE non-plug-and-play modification that must be done to the unit.** This plastic tab extends inward enough to conflict with the mounting brackets of the new LCD screen. To make the new display fit, it must be trimmed just enough to get out of the way, **but NOT completely removed as it helps support the acrylic display cover**. Use some flush cutters or a hobby knife from a good angle to remove about half of the length of this tab. It should look like [Fig 9.3](/_images/fig9-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg) when done. It's best to come at it top-down as shown, instead of inward from the back. Cutting from the back could end up removing the entire tab (this has happened to me![^3])

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig9-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig9-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig9-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

> [!NOTE]
> Are you installing in a Q20? Take a moment to make sure the jumper on the back of the board is set correctly for Q20, else the display will always be powered on when the Q20 is plugged in (whether the Q20 itself is "on" or not).<br/>

### Step 10: Insert new acrylic faceplate
With the plastic display housing facing downward, you can place the replacement acrylic front panel supplied with the new display. Note there is protective film on BOTH sides of the acrylic.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig10-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig10-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig10-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

> [!TIP]
> Avoid stripping the threaded holes in plastic: Before attempting to drive any screws, slowly turn them in the opposite direction until you hear a click and/or feel the screw drop slightly into the hole. This ensures the screw threads hole threads are aligned. You may now drive the screw into the hole, which should require very little force. This avoids cross-threading the hole, which would otherwise easily wear away the threads in the plastic and ruin the hole.

### Step 11: Place new display PCB
Fit the new display assembly into the plastic housing with it facing downward. There is also a protective film on the new display to remove before installing [Fig 11.2](/_images/fig11-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg). With the VU connector side of the PCB flush against the side of the housing, reattach the two screws on this side. Repeating a similar process of aligning with the housing, attach the two other screws near the encoder cutout. The display assembly is now ready to place back into the front bezel.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig11-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig11-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig11-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><br/><img src="/_images/fig11-4_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig11-5_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 12: Attach display assembly back into the front bezel
Basically Step 7 in reverse. Face the front bezel downward towards your work surface. While ensuring the side of the board with the encoder cutout is towards the front panel's volume knobs, carefully slide the display assembly back into the front bezel from the right side. The display assembly should fall into place when it's in the correct spot. Re-position the four smaller nylon spacers between the board and the inner lip of the front bezel as shown - they should be flush against that inner wall. Reattach four of the retention clips to hold the nylon in place.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig12-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="480"><img src="/_images/fig12-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig12-3_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 13: Reattach front panel button assembly back into the front bezel
Step 6 in reverse. Ensure the orientation is correct by lining up the passthrough hole for the power button. The long nylon spacers should be reattached along with the remaining retention clips. 

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig13-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 14: Reattach the encoder
Step 4 in reverse. While making sure the orientation is correct, push the shaft of the encoder through the hole. There is a small tab in the encoder's metal housing that should line up with the smaller holes next to the shaft's hole. While holding it in place from behind, thread the washer and 11m nut from the other side and tighten. Press fit the knob back onto the encoder shaft, respecting the position of the flat side of the D-shaft - if it sticks out too much and wont go down further, double check where the inside tabs line up with the flat side of the shaft.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig14-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 15: Reattach front bezel to mainboard/bottom plate
Reverse of Step 3. Face the front bezel towards you, but with the bottom side facing up. Your goal will be to line up the screw holes of the front bezel and bottom plate (with the bottom plate on top) while also guiding the long power button (which should still be attached to the power switch on the mainboard) through the hole in the front bezel and button PCB. After everything is aligned, reattach the center screw only. This will allow the two pieces to remain attached while you reposition the side brackets.
These brackets have a tab that slide into vertical notches in the rear panel. When inserted, treat it like a hinge and position the front of the brackets with their respective screw holes on each side. The holes of the side brackets will position below the front panel holes (so top down: Bottom Plate > Front Bezel > Side bracket). It might take some finagling to correctly seat the front of the bracket back into the front bezel, but it should sit flush when it is correct - see [Fig 15.1](/_images/fig15-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg). Reattach the appropriate screws.

<p align="center"><img src="/_images/fig15-1_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"><img src="/_images/fig15-2_jftQV2Sinst.jpg" width="240"></p>

### Step 16: Reattach all cables
Reverse of Step 2. Reattach all cables from the front panel to the mainboard. If you followed all recommendations above, the only difference will be with the 16pin display ribbon and the 8 pin VU LED cable - these should already be attached to the mainboard, and you will be attaching the display assembly side at this step. Take care to reattach the 16 pin cable with the correct orientation. At this point, minus the top cover, the Quadraverb is mostly back together. If you are comfortable and experienced with testing electronics while they are open (again, at your own risk!), you may want to test the new display at this point before you button up the unit completely. 

### Step 17: Close top cover
Slide the top cover from the back and line up the screw holes. Reattach the 6 screws removed in Step 1.

### Enjoy!

[^1]: "PH" refers to a series of wire-to-board connectors by JST Manufacturing, relating directly to the connector specifics such as housing design, mating, pin pitch (2mm in this case), electrical characteristics, etc. Alesis chose this style of connector for many of the wire-to-board connections within the Quadraverb. You can learn more from [JST's PH catalog](https://www.jst-mfg.com/product/pdf/eng/ePH.pdf?6acbb8b0386c8)

[^2]: ["IDC" (Insulation-Displacement Contact)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDC_(electrical_connector)) refers to a style of connector that can be crimped directly onto a ribbon cable. The contacts use small blades to pierce through the cable insulation to make contact with the wire inside. The ribbon-side connector is typically keyed with a tab on one side, which can avoid backwards connections when the board side uses a shrouded-style connector (or "box header") which have a matching notch on one side. Alesis used unshrouded connectors (just plain "pin headers") within the Quadraverb, which allow the ribbon cable to connect in either orientation.

[^3]: If you happen to cut off too much of the tab, that side of the acrylic cover will no longer sit straight. To work around this if it happens to you, use a small piece of clear tape where the tab was to hold the new acrylic cover in place. Align the cover to where it would have sat before taping down.
